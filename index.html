EEG Test in Rahim Yar Khan: What It Shows and When You Need One

An EEG, or electroencephalogram, is a test that records the electrical activity of the brain. It is painless, it uses no radiation, and it is done on an outpatient basis, which means no hospital stay is needed. Small sensors are placed on the scalp, and the test records the brain's signals while the patient rests. In Rahim Yar Khan, EEG is available at the National Neurophysiology Center.

The test is ordered when a neuro physician suspects fits or seizures. A fit can look the same from the outside whether it comes from epilepsy or from another cause, such as low sugar or a faint. The EEG helps the doctor find the type of epilepsy, because different types respond to different medicines. It is also used to check whether the treatment is working, and whether the medicine needs to change.

You may need an EEG if you have had a fit, a brief loss of awareness, a staring spell, or jerking of the arms and legs. It is also used when a person has unexplained episodes of confusion or blackouts that no one has been able to explain. The neuro physician decides whether the test is needed after asking about the symptoms and examining the patient.

During the test, the patient usually lies still with eyes closed, and the recording takes some time. There is nothing to fear in it: the sensors only record, they do not send anything into the brain. After the test, the neuro physician reads the recording and explains what it shows, what the diagnosis is, and what the treatment will be.

An EEG turns a guess into an answer. Without it, epilepsy is often treated by trial and error, with the wrong medicine or the wrong dose. With it, the doctor can choose the right medicine for the right type of epilepsy. If you or someone in your family has had fits, staring spells or unexplained blackouts, an EEG can give the answer.
